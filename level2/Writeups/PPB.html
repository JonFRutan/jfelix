Genesis of PPB

Privacy Preserving Blockchain (PPB) is the basis of our project.

Seniors in the VCU College of Engineering must complete a year-long capstone project. We were given a list of some 140 to pick our top 10 from, of which Privacy Preserving Blockchain was my 7th pick.

The name of the project matches the whole description; how we were to use blockchain to "preserve privacy" was entirely up to us as a team. Let me get this out of the way now: I knew nothing about working with crypto and blockchain.

While I wasn't thrilled with this project assignment, I ended up enjoying working on it and am very pleased with the result.

This writeup will detail the development and structure of this project, I promise it'll be more interesting than it may seem.

The PPB Project

We probably had the most freedom of any of the capstone projects, which proved to be a double edged sword.

  • The Good: As long as we worked with blockchain and "preserved privacy", we could pretty much do whatever we wanted.
  • The Bad: We had no initial direction, and being inexperienced with blockchain made it difficult to determine where to go.

From our ideas, we settled on the most feasible: a blockchain polling system. Polling systems are a very common use case for blockchain technology, and our faculty advisor, Hong-Sheng Zhou, has papers published on this very topic.

(Another idea was a blockchain-based domain registrar, more on this later)

Applications of Blockchain

Is blockchain actually used anywhere other than cryptocurrency? If we ignore DeFi, cryptoexchanges, and things like NFTs we see a few commercial applications:

  1. Decentralized Identity - Identity Verification Systems that don't rely on centralized databases for verification.
  2. Private, Permissioned Blockchains - Distributed Ledgers, internal private blockchain networks used for auditing of financial history and transactions.
  3. Targeted Supply Chains - Walmart uses blockchain for tracking food sources. De Beers used Tracr to trace diamonds from their source.

The biggest barrier of blockchain is its complexity and speed. There was a time where there was a lot of optimism with incorporating blockchain into global supply chains or healthcare systems. These ambitions have fallen through, with a few notorious cases.

Blockchain is:

  • GREAT for decentralizing systems and their infrastructure.
  • GREAT for publicly accountable ledgers and systems.
  • GREAT for creating private systems with cryptography.

  • AWFUL for high throughput systems and tasks.
  • AWFUL for replacing existing tech infrastructure.
  • AWFUL for storing complex or large data.

A Case For Blockchain Domain Registrars

As previously mentioned, my proposal for a project was a blockchain-backed domain registrar. Decentraweb is an example of this.

Problems with our current domain registration system:

  • Centralization - Domain registration is wholly controlled by the centralized authority ICANN.
  • Privacy (Lack thereof) - Domain registration requires owners to provide contact information, publicly available through ICANN Lookup and Whois.
  • Vulnerabilities - DNS is subject to spoofing, phishing, and cache poisoning.
  • Security - (Soon) SSL certificates must be renewed every 47 days.

A blockchain backend resolves these with decentralization, optional anonymity, and an indisputable cryptographic source of truth for domain ownership.

The issue with Decentraweb, and other web3 domain registrars, is that they are exclusively web3, selling web3-specific TLDs rather than common ones like .com.

MACI & Our Goal

The MACI Logo

MACI stands for Minimal Anti-Collusion Infrastructure. It provides the smart-contracts that make private, secure blockchain voting accessible for development. One statement from the MACI roadmap stood out:

"It's no secret that one of MACI long standing issues has [sic] having a centralised coordinator. They are able to see all of the votes in cleartext, which allows them to collude with bribers themselves, as well as voters."

"Perfect!" I said in response. Now we had a goal: do something about the MACI coordinator.

The project was no longer just another blockchain voting system, it would be about pushing the boundaries of anti-collusionary voting.

Smart Contracts

Smart Contracts establish terms of agreement between parties as actual pieces of code that exist on the blockchain — immutable and independent. MACI has a set of smart contracts written in Solidity. Notable are:

  • MACI.sol - The core contract.
  • Policy.sol - Defines the registration process.
  • PollFactory.sol - Deploys new polls, each with three corresponding contracts: Poll.sol, MessageProcessor.sol, and Tally.sol.

MACIs Coordinator

The coordinator is a backend system (NOT on the blockchain) for managing and interacting with all smart contracts via APIs. It handles user registrations, poll creation, and vote submission. Its key vulnerability: whoever controls it can see all votes in cleartext.

Where To Begin

Our original intention was to fork existing voting software. However after testing several options I ran into consistent issues: difficult setup, mandatory crypto wallets, and poor extensibility. These three issues cemented our project requirements:

  1. Make the backend extendable - Preferably written in a simpler language than TypeScript.
  2. Make it accessible - Abstract away all cryptocurrency/wallet aspects from end-users.
  3. Weaken the coordinator - Mitigate the coordinator's control and reduce its value as an attack target.

The project would no longer be a fork, instead built from the ground up on the foundations of MACI.

Jon's Encryptionary

A glossary of important terms used throughout this writeup.

  • Byzantine Fault Tolerance [BFT]

    A property of distributed systems ensuring consensus is reached by a minimum number of participants. The BFT limit: n ≥ 3f + 1.

  • Collusion

    Cooperation to cheat or deceive. In elections: coercion to vote a certain way by threat or bribery.

  • Encryption

    Similar to a hash, but reversible. Two-way.

  • Hash

    A one-way function turning any input into nondescript text. One-way, irreversible.

  • BabyJubJub

    An elliptic curve built for zk-SNARK circuits. Its field prime matches Groth16's, making elliptic curve operations cheap inside proofs. Every voter keypair in PPB uses BabyJubJub.

  • ECDH

    Elliptic Curve Diffie-Hellman. Lets two parties unlock the same secret without sharing the key. Every vote is ECDH-encrypted with the coordinator's public key.

  • Groth16

    A zk-SNARK proving scheme creating the smallest possible proofs. MACI uses it to generate and verify all ZKPs.

  • bcrypt

    Intentionally slow password hashing used in auth.py. Based on the Blowfish cipher, with salting and configurable slowness to mitigate brute-force attacks.

Weakening The Coordinator

The coordinator is MACI's Achilles' Heel. The simplest solution: keep the decryption key out of the coordinator until the moment of decryption — and even then, split it using Threshold Key Cryptography.

A diagram showing a key being split into three pieces, then distributed among 3 computers.

This is a Guardian Node system. Independent nodes hold pieces of the split key. The coordinator requests pieces only when ready to finalize, and nodes may choose to provide their share. With this system, votes cannot be read until consensus is reached among guardian nodes after the poll has finalized.

Our coordinator consists of three services: The Backend (communicates with smart contracts, guardians, and clients), The Guardians (nodes holding split key shares), and The Threshold (splits the private key into k pieces).

The Frontend

A React/Vite webapp in TypeScript with three sections: the user interface, the admin interface, and an interactive demo for our expo.

The PPB login page.

The PPB admin panel.

The frontend handles critical privacy functionality: a persistent MACI Identity Keypair (BabyJubJub — private key never leaves the client) and a per-vote ephemeral keypair that removes linkability between votes.

What about the crypto wallets?

They're gone. Everything crypto related is handled by the backend. Voting on PPB Polling requires not an ounce of knowledge on cryptocurrency.

The Private Half of the Identity Keypair

The private half of the identity keypair never leaves the client by design. Sending it to the server would let any actor who gains access decrypt all votes. Keeping it client-side means a lost key loses an account — a far better outcome than a compromised voting system.

Potential upgrade: Keep the private key encrypted locally, decrypted only when the user enters their password.

Custodial Wallets

We moved all wallet responsibility to the backend, creating a contained, managed wallet in association with every user account. Using Python's eth-account and Web3.py libraries, a keypair is generated per user and kept perpetually funded from the deployer wallet — so users can always participate without knowing anything about cryptocurrency.

Diagram of account creation with associated custodial wallet.

Zero-Knowledge Proofs & zk-SNARKS

A zero-knowledge proof lets a prover demonstrate knowledge of something without revealing the thing itself. MACI uses zk-SNARKs (Zero-Knowledge Succinct Non-Interactive ARguments of Knowledge) — specifically Groth16 — to verify votes are legitimate without revealing who voted for what.

The math rests on computationally asymmetric problems (easy to verify, hard to reverse), polynomial representations of statements, and non-interactivity via the Fiat-Shamir transform (hashing the transcript instead of requiring a live verifier). For deeper reading: Survey, arXiv, NIST.

The Backend

Primarily Python with FastAPI. Key libraries: SQLAlchemy (SQLite), Web3 & eth-account (Ethereum), python-jose & passlib (JWT & bcrypt), uvicorn (web server).

The Coordinator Keypair is divided by SSS and distributed to guardians. The backend cannot read votes until the threshold is met, after which the key is reassembled, used, then destroyed.

Summary Of Each File
  • main.py - FastAPI entry point.
  • auth.py - Password hashing (bcrypt), JWT creation/decoding.
  • database.py - SQLAlchemy engine and DB calls.
  • models.py - 5 tables: User, CustodialWallet, Poll, TallyResult, VoteReceipt.
  • wallet_service.py - Custodial wallet creation and funding; MACI signups, poll joining, vote publishing.
  • tally_pipeline.py - Off-chain proof generation pipeline. Temporarily holds the MACI coordinator private key.
  • routes_threshold.py - SSS key splitting, guardian management, and key reconstruction triggering.
  • routes_guardians.py - Heartbeat signals, active node listing, ceremony checking.

Shamir's Secret Sharing

A secret splitting algorithm where a secret cannot be revealed unless a predefined threshold of shares is met. Properties: information-theoretically secure, minimal share size, extensible, and flexible (variable shares per participant).

The math: a polynomial of degree k-1 is uniquely determined by k points (Lagrange Interpolation). The secret is the polynomial's value at x=0. Shares are points on the polynomial; any k of them reconstruct it. Division inside the bounded field prime uses modular inverses via Fermat's Little Theorem: a⁻¹ = a^(p-2) % p.

The Threshold

Runs in four stages: Setup (splits the coordinator private key into n encrypted shares), Storage (each guardian receives and re-encrypts their share locally with PBKDF2), Open Ceremony (guardians detect and submit to active ceremonies), Reassembly (on threshold met: checksum verification → polynomial verification → key reconstruction → decryption → key destruction).

Only standard library Python is used (hashlib, secrets, base64, urllib) to protect against supply-chain risks.

The Guardians

Independent nodes each holding a piece of the split key. They operate on a 6-second loop: send a heartbeat, check for open ceremonies, and submit their share if one is found. Extensible for human oversight, poll validity checking, or peer-to-peer coordination before submission.

Rapidsnark

Rapidsnark is a Groth16 prover that made proof generation at least 2500× faster than the default snarkjs — turning multi-minute timeouts into negligible waits during testing.

The Development Environment

MACI requires significant setup (pnpm deps, ~8GB zkeys, coordinator keypair, contract deployment). All of this is automated via a Makefile with commands like make dev, make guardians, make backend-setup, and make clean.

Terminus of PPB

The original problem statement asked us to apply cryptographic techniques to blockchain to keep data private yet provably correct. PPB Polling fulfills this: it identified a real vulnerability in the MACI coordinator and established a concrete mitigation through threshold key distribution and guardian nodes.
